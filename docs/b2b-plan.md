# 10IS for Business: plan

*Hevini Sporting, November 2026. Research notes and sources are summarised in the appendix.*

## The one-paragraph version

10IS already sits at the moment a player decides what string goes in their racket next. The plan is to make that moment the home of tennis equipment. Stringers run their shop on 10IS. String companies reach players who genuinely fit their strings. Retailers and racket brands plug into the same decision. Every side pays for something different. **The AI's main pick is never for sale.** That trust is what every paying side is really buying, so the whole model is built so money can sit next to the recommendation but never inside it.

We win **stringers first, for free**, because their bookings and job records create the data and the trust that brands pay for. **Sample programmes**, where brands pay stringers to install trial sets, are the bridge: they are useful to brands from day one and give stringers a reason to upgrade. **Insights subscriptions** become the main brand revenue once there is enough volume to report anonymously. **Sponsored placements and affiliate links** are real but small, and are designed to stay small and honest.

## 1. What we learned from everyone who tried this

| What was tried | Who | What happened | What we take from it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stringer job-tracking apps | String Maven (~$5/yr), Nevalto (from $15), RacquetBuddy | All small; individual stringers pay very little | Stringer software alone is a weak business. Give it away and make money elsewhere |
| Club stringing and demo software | RacquetPass ($99–$150/mo, 200+ clubs incl. Life Time) | The only stringer software with visible paying customers | Clubs and multi-stringer shops pay; solo stringers don't. A future "Club" tier makes sense |
| Booking built into club platforms | CourtReserve (stringing in beta) | Big platforms are adding stringing as a feature | Don't compete on job tracking. Compete on the player link: our players, their bags, their reminders |
| Player-to-stringer marketplaces | Stringr (LA, 50+ stringers), Stringbox ($19/job + $10/mo), Direct Tennis, ServiceLine, Racket Ready, StringIt | All small or stalled. Demand is very local and infrequent (a few restrings a year), and pickup or mail-in eats the margin | Don't run logistics. Players drop off at their own stringer; we fill the queue and keep both sides informed |
| Mobile bike repair | Velofix (franchise, sold to Backcountry in 2026), Beeline (became shop scheduling software, sold 2025) | Service vans became a retailer's service arm; the software outlived the vans | Value sits in the software that links service to equipment purchases, not the vans |
| Bike shop booking software | Workshop by Bikebook (£49.99–£99.99/mo, $89 US) | Growing; the closest analogue | Shops pay for booking that brings them customers. Price our Pro tier well below this to start |
| Brand engagement on a sports app | Strava sponsored challenges (from $30,000) | Core B2B revenue | Brands pay for reach and engagement with the right athletes |
| Selling data alone | Strava Metro (data for cities) | Hard to sell, now free | Insights work as part of a brand relationship (reach, samples, catalogue), not as a standalone data product |
| Permissioned equipment data | Arccos (golf), with PING, TaylorMade, Callaway and Titleist as investors and partners | Fitters see a golfer's data only with permission; brands get aggregate insights | Share data only with consent, aggregate and anonymise. That is our model |
| Venue listing plus insights | Untappd for Business (~$90–$120/mo) | Established monthly SaaS | Businesses pay monthly for a listing and analytics when the audience is real |
| Retail affiliate in tennis | Amazon 3%, Pro:Direct 3% (1-day cookie), Tennis Express ~5%, Tennis Warehouse (rate not published, exclusivity required, applications paused) | Thin margins. TW's exclusivity clause clashes with a neutral app | Affiliate links are a convenience for players and a small revenue line. Never take an exclusive deal |
| Brand stringer programmes | Luxilon, Solinco, Babolat and others | No public programme or price. Brands reach stringers through reps, associations (USRSA ~$129, ERSA) and pro-tour teams | **Open gap.** A measurable "pay stringers to install trial sets" programme doesn't exist. We build it |
| Objective string data | Tennis Warehouse University lab database (owned by a retailer) | The incumbent "neutral" data, from a seller | Our ratings come from verified bookings, not from a retailer |

**Patterns:** service marketplaces fail on local liquidity and logistics. Software for small businesses has a low price ceiling. What makes money in this category is (a) businesses paying to reach the right customers and (b) brands paying to get products into hands in a way they can measure. 10IS has something none of the above had: **the player's decision, the player's racket history, and the stringer who carries it out, all in one place.**

## 2. What each side gets and why they'd pay

### Players: Free, Pro ($6.99/mo or $49/yr), Club ($12.99/mo or $99/yr), Pro Lifetime ($149 founder offer)
- **Get:** an honest AI string pick, book their stringer in two taps when the reminder fires, a live status ("on the machine", "ready for pickup"), every job written into their racket history automatically, and free sample restrings from brands.
- **Why it works:** it removes texting and guessing, and the reminder turns into an action. **Paid players never see sponsored placements**, which also makes the paid plans better.
- **Why these prices:** the original $4.99 one-time Pro and $1.99/mo Club priced 10IS like a utility and produced almost no recurring revenue. Benchmarks: Strava $11.99/mo or $79.99/yr, SwingVision about $14.99/mo or $149.99/yr (video AI), Arccos $199.99/yr. 10IS sits below video-AI tools and alongside training apps, with annual as the default (about 40% cheaper than monthly) because annual plans retain far better. Lifetime at about 3x annual is a time-limited founder offer, not a permanent tier. Early one-time buyers keep Pro for good.

### Stringers: Shop (free), Shop Pro ($39/mo) and Shop Club ($99/mo)
- **Free:** public shop page with online booking, a one-handed job queue, status updates players see, walk-ins, your own history per customer racket. Up to 20 app bookings a month, unlimited walk-ins.
- **Pro:**
  - unlimited bookings;
  - the racket's **full string history from every stringer**, when the player agrees;
  - **rebook prompts** when customers' strings are due (they see a one-tap "book with you again");
  - **paid sample installs** from brands, with the stringer keeping 80% of the install fee;
  - shop insights: regulars, revenue, top strings, repeat rate.
- **Why they'd pay:** one extra booking a month pays for Pro ($20–$40 labour per job). Two sample installs a month pay for it outright (about $14.40 each to the stringer). Pro is priced at about a fifth of Workshop by Bikebook and RacquetPass. Stringers decide on time saved and customers gained, not features.
- **What we never sell to shops:** ranking. The shop directory isn't sorted by plan. Pro gets a "Verified shop" badge, not position.

### String companies: Listing (free), Insights ($490/mo), Partner ($1,990/mo), sponsored placements (pay per click)
- **Listing (free):** verified brand page, manage the catalogue (names, gauges, prices, descriptions), run sponsored campaigns, campaign reporting. Lab scores and stiffness are set by 10IS, not the brand, because they drive the fit check.
- **Insights:**
  - **demand:** how often the AI recommends your strings compared with the category, week by week, by level and playstyle;
  - **funnel:** recommended → clicked → booked → installed → rated;
  - **retention:** what share of players restring with you next time, and which brands switchers go to;
  - **ratings** from verified bookings;
  - regions and the frames you're paired with.
  - Pseudonymised (players are a keyed hash) and aggregated; no figure, including a single week on a chart, is shown below 5 players.
- **Partner:** everything in Insights plus **sample programmes run through partner stringers** (the brand pays an install fee per trial set; we track sample → rating → rebuy), and a quarterly category report.
- **Sponsored placement:** one clearly labelled slot, shown only to players the string genuinely fits, after the AI's pick. Pay per click from $1.00, with a monthly cap.
- **Why they'd pay:** brands currently spend on reps, demo reels and trade promotions with no way to measure whether a stringer or player switched. We sell the one thing they can't buy anywhere else: **measured trial and switching**, at the moment of decision, with a neutral recommendation in front of it.

### Retailers: free listing plus affiliate commission
- **Smallest credible first step:** a retailer lists prices and stock for strings in our catalogue. Offers only go live once 10IS has verified the retailer, and every product link must be on the retailer's own website, because 10IS redirects players to it. Where-to-buy lists are **sorted cheapest-in-stock first, never by commission**. Links carry `rel="sponsored"` and a click code; sales come back by postback and earn 3–8% commission. Retailers see clicks, orders and revenue per string.
- **Why they'd join:** zero cost, and buyers who already know exactly what they want.

### Racket brands: Insights ($490/mo)
- **Smallest credible first step:** a verified brand page plus **frame insights**: which strings the AI pairs with your frames, the level mix of players using them, and how many players have your frame in their bag (k ≥ 5).
- **Why they'd pay:** string pairing is part of how a frame performs, and nobody measures it today. **Next step (not built):** demo-racket programmes through partner stringers, run the same way as string samples.

## 3. Revenue models and prices

| Line | Who pays | Price | How it's billed | Integrity guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shop Pro | Stringer | $39/mo | Stripe subscription | Doesn't buy ranking |
| Shop Club | Clubs, chains | $99/mo, up to 5 stringers (sold directly for now) | Invoice | Same |
| Sponsored placement | String company | CPC from $1.00 (typical $1.20–$2.00), monthly cap | Each billable click is recorded in `campaign_events`. **Invoicing is not built yet**: monthly invoices from these records come next | Fit check first; max one; labelled; never replaces or reorders; paid players exempt |
| Insights | String company, racket brand | $490/mo | Stripe subscription | k-anonymity 5; hashed players; no personal data |
| Partner (samples) | String company | $1,990/mo + install fee per sample ($15–$25) + the string | Subscription via Stripe; install fees invoiced manually at first. **Stringer payouts are not built yet** (Stripe Connect) | Samples labelled to players and stringers; sampled installs and ratings tagged and kept out of organic insights |
| Sample install margin | String company | 20% of the install fee | Netted from payouts | Same |
| Affiliate | Retailer | 3–8% of order | Postback, 30-day window | Sorted by price; commission never shown or used to rank |
| Player plans | Player | Pro $6.99/mo or $49/yr; Club $12.99/mo or $99/yr; Lifetime $149 founder offer | Stripe subscriptions | Paid players are ad-free |

**Pricing logic:**
- Shop Pro at $39 is still about one or two extra restrings a month, and well under RacquetPass ($99–$149) and bike-shop booking software ($89); Shop Club matches the club benchmark.
- Insights at $490 is an easy first yes for a small brand before 10IS has much data, and well under a single trade show.
- Partner is priced so one 200-unit sample programme costs a brand about $5,000 all-in for 200 measured trials. That is cheaper per trial than demo reels sent to shops that may never install them.
- Sponsored CPC is set high enough that brands only bid on players they fit. The minimum keeps spam out.

## 4. Which side to win first, and how (cold start)

**Order:** players (we have them) → stringers (free) → string companies (samples first, insights next, sponsored last) → retailers → racket brands.

1. **Turn the existing player base into stringer invitations.** Players already save their stringer in 10IS. When a reminder fires and their stringer isn't on 10IS, we show "Ask {stringer} to take bookings on 10IS" with a share link to `/business`. Every invited stringer arrives with at least one customer waiting.
2. **Make the stringer tool useful with zero app customers.** Walk-ins, the queue, machine mode and customer history are valuable even if no player ever books online. That is what Workshop by Bikebook and RacquetPass prove. A shop that uses the queue for walk-ins creates job data from day one.
3. **Go deep in a few cities, not wide.** Liquidity is local (Stringr's lesson). Pick two or three metros where 10IS has the most players. Sign 20–30 stringers each, plus one club per metro (the RacquetPass customer profile).
4. **Use sample programmes as the bridge to brands.** A brand doesn't need a big audience to fund 200 trial sets; it needs to know they were installed and rated. We start with one or two **design-partner brands** on Partner for three months at a discount, in exchange for complete catalogue data and one funded programme. Each install pays a Pro stringer about $14.40, which is the clearest reason for a stringer to upgrade.
5. **Turn on Insights when the data supports it.** Insights only become useful once most groups pass the 5-player floor, which in practice means roughly 10,000 recommendations a month. Until then, design partners get a monthly report rather than a self-serve dashboard.
6. **Sponsored placements last.** They need volume and they are the riskiest to trust, so they come after the integrity story is proven publicly.
7. **Retailers and racket brands join the same rails.** Retail offers make "where to buy" work for players. Racket brands buy frame insights once string-pairing data exists.

## 5. Simple unit economics

*Prices come from section 3. Conversion inputs are working assumptions, not measured or researched figures, so treat them as levers to test: a sponsored slot shown on 35% of free-plan setups, an 8% click-through rate, 15% of retailer click-outs buying, a $20–$22 basket, a 5% average commission, and 4% of active players paying (consumer-app freemium norms run roughly 2–5%). If any is off by half, sponsored and affiliate revenue move by half; the subscription lines don't depend on them.*

**Scenario A: one metro, year one**
- 3,000 active players and 1,500 recommendations a month.
- 30 shops, 8 on Pro.
- 1 design-partner brand on Partner at 50% off for 3 months, then full price.

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Shop Pro: 8 × $39 | $312 |
| Partner (design-partner rate): $995 + 40 sample installs × $18 × 20% margin | $1,139 |
| Sponsored: 1,275 free-plan views × 35% fit fill × 8% CTR × $1.40 | ≈ $50 |
| Affiliate: 150 click-outs × 15% conversion × $20 × 5% | ≈ $23 |
| **B2B total** | **≈ $1,520/mo** |
| Player subscriptions: 3,000 × 4% paid × ~$55/yr blended | ≈ $550 |

This is a learning-stage metro. It proves bookings, samples and integrity; it doesn't pay salaries.

**Scenario B: national, about year three**
- 60,000 active players and 30,000 recommendations a month.
- 800 shops, 220 on Pro; 15 clubs.
- 6 string companies (2 Insights, 4 Partner); 2 racket brands on Insights; 10 retailers.
- 8 sample programmes a year of 200 units each.

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Shop Pro 220 × $39 + Shop Club 15 × $99 | $10,065 |
| Insights (2 string + 2 racket) × $490 | $1,960 |
| Partner 4 × $1,990 | $7,960 |
| Sample margin: about 133 installs/mo × $18 × 20% | $480 |
| Sponsored: 25,500 free views × 35% fill × 8% CTR × $1.50 | ≈ $1,070 |
| Affiliate: 3,000 click-outs × 15% × $22 × 5% | ≈ $500 |
| **B2B total** | **≈ $22,000/mo (≈ $264k ARR)** |
| Player subscriptions: 60,000 × 4% paid × ~$55/yr blended | ≈ $11,000/mo (≈ $132k ARR) |
| **Total** | **≈ $33,000/mo (≈ $396k ARR)** |

**What the scenarios show:**
- **Brand subscriptions are about half of B2B revenue; sponsored and affiliate together are under 10%.** That is deliberate: the business doesn't depend on the slot that could compromise trust.
- **Costs to serve are low.** Hosting and email are cents per shop, and Stripe takes about 3%. The real cost is sales: about $150 of field time per signed stringer (payback about 5 months on Pro) and founder-led brand sales on 2–3 month cycles.
- **Upside not counted:** in-app payment for bookings (a 1–2% take on $40–$70 jobs), a Club tier for chains, demo-racket programmes, and international metros through ERSA-member stringers.

## 6. Recommendation integrity (the main risk)

If players suspect the pick is bought, they stop trusting it. Then brands have nothing to buy and stringers lose the bookings it drives. So the integrity rules are enforced in code, covered by tests, and published at `/how-we-rank`.

| Rule | How it's enforced in the product |
|---|---|
| The AI pick is never for sale | The recommendation is generated and stored before any sponsor code runs. The sponsored slot is a **separate request** that reads a deep-frozen copy and returns its own object (`shared/sponsor-fit.ts`, `GET /api/recommendation-runs/:id/sponsored`). A test proves a maximum-bid sponsor leaves the pick, crosses, alternatives (and their order) and explanation unchanged, and that serving and clicking the slot doesn't modify the stored run |
| Only shown when it genuinely fits | Hard rules match the technician's own safety logic: no stiff poly for elbow, shoulder or wrist issues; no stiff poly in RA 68+ frames; no poly for slow swings unless the technician chose poly; only string types the technician itself recommended. Then a fit score of at least 70. **Money only breaks ties among strings that already fit** |
| Brands can't game the fit check | Stiffness and the 1–10 scores are editorial and set only by 10IS (admin route). Brand edits to them are rejected. New strings show "specs pending" and are treated conservatively (not shown to injured players) |
| Never sell the pick back | If the sponsored string is the AI's pick, no slot is shown and no one is charged |
| At most one, always after, always labelled | One slot, rendered after the technician's note and the alternatives, with a prominent "Sponsored · Paid placement by {brand}" label, shading, and "Why am I seeing this?" listing the fit reasons |
| Brands can narrow, never widen | Level targeting only removes players |
| Paid players are ad-free | `adFree` entitlement; the endpoint returns nothing |
| Brands see what they can't buy | "Fit reach" shows a brand, before spending, what share of recent players its string qualifies for and why it's held back for the rest |
| Shops can't pay for position | The directory isn't ordered by plan |
| Affiliate never changes order | Offers are sorted by stock, then price; commission isn't exposed or used |
| Ratings are real | Only players with a ready or collected booking can rate. Ratings feed insights only above 5 players. We never buy, condition or suppress reviews. Installs and ratings from free samples are tagged `sampled` and kept out of a string's organic ratings and funnel; brands see them only in their sample results |
| Only verified brands reach players | Unverified brands can build drafts but can't run sponsored placements, and serving skips them even if a row says "active". Retail links need a verified retailer and must sit on its own domain; the redirect re-checks both on every click |

**Governance**
- The integrity tests run in GitHub Actions on every pull request (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`). To make them **block** merges, mark the "check-and-test" job as a required check in branch protection; that's a repository setting and isn't switched on yet.
- `campaign_events` records the fit score of every impression and click for audit.
- We publish the share of setups that showed a sponsored slot each quarter.
- Any brand found pressuring stringers or players loses sponsored access.
- One switch (`status = paused` on all campaigns) turns every sponsored slot off.

### FTC, EU and UK checklist

| Requirement | Source | Our implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Paid content in AI output must be clearly separated from organic output | FTC staff guidance on generative AI (2023); chatbot guidance | Separate request, separate card, after the pick, never inside it |
| Paid placement visually distinct with a clear text label, close and prominent, before engagement | FTC 2013 search-engine letter; .com Disclosures; Native Advertising guide (2015) | Shaded card, "Sponsored" label top-left at body size, brand named, visible before tapping, same on every device |
| Disclose material connections, including affiliate links and free product | Endorsement Guides 16 CFR 255 (2023) | "10IS may earn a commission…" next to every buy link; `rel="sponsored"`; brand-funded samples labelled "sponsored by {brand}" to players and stringers |
| No fake, bought, conditional or suppressed reviews; no company-controlled "independent" reviews | 16 CFR 465 (2024), up to $53,088 per violation | Ratings only from verified bookings; no incentives tied to sentiment; no suppression; method described at `/how-we-rank` |
| Disclose paid ranking and the main ranking parameters | EU UCPD Annex I 11a, Art. 7(4a) (Omnibus) | `/how-we-rank` explains the AI, the fit check, the sponsored slot and shop and retail ordering |
| Ad transparency: that it's an ad, who paid, why it's shown | EU DSA Art. 26 (small enterprises may be exempt; we comply anyway) | "Paid placement by {brand}" plus "Why am I seeing this?" with fit reasons |
| No targeting on special-category data | DSA Art. 26(3), GDPR | Injury information is used only to *exclude* unsafe sponsored strings, never to target; brands can't target by injury |
| Ads obviously identifiable | UK CAP Code 2.1; DMCC Act 2024 (up to 10% of turnover) | Same label; no "collab"-style euphemisms |
| Aggregate and protect shared data; consent for personal data | GDPR; Arccos precedent | Insights use only `string_signals`: no user id, a keyed hash per player (pseudonymous, so still personal data under GDPR and never exported raw), and no figure below 5 players. A stringer sees a racket's full history only on the player's per-booking opt-in, and never the player's private notes |

## 7. Other risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Stringers don't adopt (low willingness to pay, habit) | High | Free tier is useful with walk-ins alone; invitations come from their own customers; paid sample installs |
| Club platforms (CourtReserve) bundle stringing | Medium | Integrate rather than compete: our edge is the player's bag, reminder and recommendation. Export and ICS already exist; add a club API next |
| Brands won't pay until volume exists | High | Samples first (useful at small scale); design-partner pricing; monthly reports before self-serve dashboards |
| A brand pushes a stringer to recommend its string | Medium | Samples labelled to players; stringer payouts per install, not per rating; ratings tracked separately for sampled installs |
| Insights leak personal data | Low | No ids in signals; every figure, including each chart week, needs 5+ players; no raw export; signal writes never block player requests |
| Click fraud against a rival's campaign | Medium | Clicks bill once per recommendation and only for a slot actually shown; recommendations are rate-limited. Next: per-user and per-IP click caps and refunds for flagged patterns before invoicing |
| A lookalike brand or a phishing link | Medium | Verification before anything reaches players; retail links locked to the retailer's own domain and re-checked on every redirect |
| Sponsored inventory too small to matter | High | Planned for: sponsored is under 10% of revenue in both scenarios |
| Liquidity is local and thin | High | Deep-city launch; walk-in value; reminders create demand at the right moment |
| Payment disputes on bookings | Low (no in-app payment yet) | Pay at the shop for now; add Stripe Connect with clear refund rules later |

## 8. What's built in this release

- **Stringers:**
  - public shop page (`/shops/:slug`) and directory (`/shops`);
  - online booking with a drop-off day, rush option and consent to share history;
  - one-handed queue at `/shop`: big tension readout, one thumb-reachable next-step button per job, machine mode, walk-ins;
  - live status for players at `/bookings`;
  - completed jobs written into the player's bag automatically;
  - customer history with consent;
  - rebook prompts and insights on Pro;
  - Shop Pro via Stripe test mode (fake Stripe in tests).
- **String companies:**
  - brand portal at `/brand`: profile, catalogue with locked lab specs, sponsored campaigns with fit reach, sample programmes, insights dashboard on demo data;
  - Insights and Partner plans via Stripe test mode.
- **Retailers:** price and stock offers (verified retailers, own-domain links only), tracked `/api/go/offer/:id` redirects, conversion postback, click and sales reporting.
- **Racket brands:** brand page plus frame-pairing insights.
- **Players:**
  - "Book a restring" from the reminder, prefilled with last time's shop and setup;
  - "Book this setup" and "Where to buy" on the recommendation;
  - claim free sample restrings;
  - rate the job.
- **Disclosure:** `/how-we-rank` and `/business`.

**Next:**
- Club tier.
- In-app payment through Stripe Connect.
- Invite-your-stringer flow from saved stringers.
- Demo-racket programmes.
- Monthly CPC invoicing from `campaign_events`, with click-fraud caps.
- Stringer payouts for sample installs (Stripe Connect).
- An admin screen for verifying brands (today it's one admin API call: `PATCH /api/admin/brands/:id/verify`).
- Make the CI check required in branch protection.
- Quarterly published sponsored-share figure.
